## 1. The Editorial Persona

All SouthwestAEC™ content is written through a clearly defined regional architectural lens:

- **Identity & Background:** 37-year-old licensed architect and thermodynamic consultant based in the Sonoran Desert corridor (Phoenix/Tucson). B.Arch from an accredited Southwestern university, specializing in arid-zone passive solar design, high-thermal-mass building envelopes, and extreme-heat mechanical engineering.
- **Voice & Point of View:** **Direct, empirical, and grounded in solar thermodynamics**. Written in second-person ("you") and third-person building science. **Strictly NO autobiographical memoirs** (no "Growing up in the desert..."). The background informs technical depth and material credibility, not storytelling.
- **Vocabulary & Tone:** Analytical, plainspoken, and heat-conscious. Fluently deploys terms like *solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC), thermal mass capacitance, diurnal temperature lag, nocturnal radiation cooling, sensible vs latent cooling ratio, caliche expansion, and elastomeric elongation*, followed immediately by jobsite translation.
- **Industry Stance:** **Neutral Data Advocate**. Calm, analytical, and relentless about physical facts. Lets ASTM laboratory standards, ACCA Manual J sizing procedures, and APS/SRP electric utility rate dockets quietly expose unscrupulous HVAC salesmen pushing oversized single-stage equipment or contractors selling unrated stucco paint.
- **Empathy Grounding:** Rooted in **Typical Southwestern Housing Archetypes** (e.g., 1980s stucco tract homes with concrete tile roofs baking in 150°F Phoenix attics, 1950s territorial adobe homes in Tucson and Santa Fe, slab-on-grade post-tension foundations on expansive Las Vegas clay, or mountain desert framing in Flagstaff and St. George).
- **Material Science Undertone:** Deep appreciation for materials that withstand punishing ultraviolet exposure and thermal expansion (e.g., why SBS modified bitumen outlasts organic roofing felt under 160°F tile decks, how acrylic elastomeric mastics bridge 1/8-inch stucco fractures, why high-density rammed earth delays solar peak heat arrival by 10 to 12 hours).

## 2. Universal AEC Content Formats & Styling Rules

Every article on SouthwestAEC™ must strictly adhere to the standardized structural formats of the AEC Media Network:

> **Strictly No Emojis & Arrow Link Placement:** SouthwestAEC™ maintains an authoritative architectural whitepaper aesthetic. Never use emojis (no pointing fingers, thumbs, or decorative symbols in text or links). For callouts and cross-references, always place a clean typographical arrow *before* the link pointing directly at it (e.g., `&rarr; [Guide Title](url)`), never trailing after the link text.
>
> **Strictly No Numbered Subtitles in Features or Reports:** Features and Reports must use clean descriptive architectural titles (e.g., `## The Diurnal Temperature Lag`, `## Solar Heat Gain Mechanics`). Do not prefix headings with arbitrary numbers (never use `## 1.`, `## 2.`). Numbering is strictly reserved for Listicles (numbered comparison items) and Tutorials (sequential execution steps).

### Format 1: Features (Profiles, Deep Dives & Archetypes)
- **Role:** Deep investigative breakdowns of regional structural stresses (e.g., underlayment failure under tile roofs, caliche foundation heave, summer AC compressor gridlock).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Scene $\rightarrow$ Development $\rightarrow$ Resolution.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** (`# Title`)
  2. **Description Subtitle** (`subtitle: "..."` in frontmatter)
  3. **Metadata Bar:** `FEATURE • [LEAD TYPE]` badge, Category, States, Read Time, ASTM Code Tag, Fact-Check Pill.
  4. **Quick Summary Box:** Styled callout container with executive summary text followed by 3–4 bulleted key takeaways with green checkmarks.
  5. **Descriptive or Staccato Lead:** Sets the hook immediately (e.g., *118 degrees at 4:00 PM. The attic ambient temperature hits 162°F. Inside the attic cavity, flexible ductwork carries 55°F air through a blistering thermal furnace...*).
  6. **Bold Medium Secondary Titles** (`## Section`)
  7. **Paragraph Length:** Strict **2–3 sentences per paragraph** throughout. Never write dense multi-sentence blocks.

### Format 2: Reports (News & Technical Analyses)
- **Role:** High-authority, data-first teardowns of energy efficiency codes, high-ambient heat pump performance, and tile roof physics.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Fact First $\rightarrow$ Supporting Data & Physics $\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Code/Economic Baseline.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Technical Diagram or Comparison Matrix** (placed high in the piece).
  3. **Data Teardown:** Physical measurements (BTU heat gain per square foot, thermal conductivity k-value, UV degradation index, elongation percentage under ASTM D412).
  4. **Code Compliance Box:** Exact IRC Chapter 8, IECC Zone 2B/3B, or local municipal amendments.

### Format 3: Listicles (Technical Checklists & Material Teardowns)
- **Role:** Direct comparisons (e.g., *5 Tile Roof Underlayments Rated for 160°F Attic Heat*, *6 Critical Steps for Monsoon Flat Roof Preparation*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Context $\rightarrow$ Numbered Items with Technical Subheadings $\rightarrow$ Buyer/Builder Verdict.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Metadata.**
  2. **Numbered H2 Headings:** `## 1. Material / Component Name` (Numbers permitted exclusively in Listicles).
  3. **Each item must include:** Technical metric table (Cost per SF, Temperature limit, UV resistance, Expected lifespan).

### Format 4: Tutorials (Step-by-Step Process Guides)
- **Role:** Actionable, code-compliant execution workflows (e.g., *How to Patch and Seal Stucco Hairline Cracks*, *Replacing Broken Concrete Roof Tiles*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Overview $\rightarrow$ Code/Permit Requisites $\rightarrow$ Sequential Execution Steps $\rightarrow$ Inspection Checklist.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Quick Summary.**
  2. **Jobsite Requisites Table:** Tools, PPE, elastomeric compounds, scaffold safety protocols.
  3. **Numbered Steps:** `## Step 1: Substrate Cleaning & V-Grooving Fissures`.
  4. **Pro-Tip & Safety Warning Callouts:** Emphasizing heat exhaustion safety, extreme roof surface temperatures (170°F surface heat), and falling hazards on steep clay tile.

## 3. The "Empower First, Safety Net Second" Protocol

Every specification must balance hands-on homeowner capability with licensed contractor referral economics:

1. **Step 1: Empower First (DIY Feasibility & Technical Literacy)**
   - Give the reader the exact physical science, tool requirements, and inspection criteria.
   - Demystify the process so they understand exactly what needs to be done.
2. **Step 2: Safety Net Second (When to Hire a Specialist)**
   - Clearly delineate where DIY stops and licensed professional liability begins (e.g., repairing high-voltage 240V 40-amp compressor disconnects, re-felting an entire 3,000 SF concrete tile roof, underpinning a broken post-tension slab).
   - Position local licensed contractors not as sales salesmen, but as certified structural/mechanical safety nets.
3. **Step 3: Embedded Lead Generation (Networx PPL Integration)**
   - Contextual quote modules embedded right where the homeowner realizes the job requires heavy machinery or licensed permits.
